**What the AI did well**
- **Measure before deciding, and say the number.** The "teleporting" robots were diagnosed by sampling the pelvis bone of every clip (the jog clip carries +1,061 cm of travel in the pelvis while the root never moves). The railing the owner wanted to jump was measured before choosing a vault: top 127 cm, the jump reaches 204 cm, but under that corridor's 300 cm ceiling the player's feet top out at 120 cm, 7 cm short "for ever". The crates' gaps came from stacking 105 cm apart a crate that is exactly 100 cm high (read from its vertices). The pack's wall stair "belongs to the wall" because the pack has a wall piece with no teeth on one side and a sill at 1,214 cm, where the author's stair arrives at 1,215.
- **Checks that can fail, proven to fail.** The ice-wall fix was run on the old code and on the new one with the same new check: old, "base 314 cm from the feet" (the wall on the roof); new, "-2 cm from the feet". The enemy animation check (pelvis within 60 cm of the capsule) read 500 to 1,000 cm on the old code and 16 to 35 cm on the fix.
- **Using the kit's own pieces instead of improvising.** The hand ladder the owner wanted is part of a railed platform of the Deep Water Station pack (found by photographing twelve candidates in the game); the castle's wall stair arrives at the pack's own stair piece of the wall.
- **Reviews that found real faults.** Sol 6.1 reproduced numerically a two-frame wobble in the chief's first grip fix, and found that the shield's smoke check "never fired a real trace at its hit box, so it passed without one".
- **Overnight work with nobody to ask.** The enemies chat worked while the owner slept (his order: work in batches, do not stop, questions go to the advisor, not to him), coordinated with the map chat by message, and left a package where all four fights start, clear, set their flags and drop the two Power Cells.
- **Pixel art that is also light.** The castle rebuilt as plain blocks went from 45.7 M placed triangles to 1.68 M, no Nanite, 32 px tiles, and the castle pack's share of the cook from 1,172 MB to 84 MB.

**Where the AI failed and how we fixed it** (the part judges value most in a dev log)
- **The first maps were rejected outright.** At 00:53 the owner wrote (Spanish): *"se hizo un prototipo de mapa para ambos mundos. Ninguno sirve en nada, elimina ambos. Se dieron assets que se usaron indiscriminadamente y al azar"* ("a map prototype was made for both worlds; neither is of any use, delete both; the assets were used indiscriminately and at random"), and then *"absolutamente nada de los últimos 2 mapas hechos sirve para nada. Son completamente inútiles y un trabajo pésimo"* ("absolutely nothing of the last two maps is of any use; they are completely useless and terrible work"). The architect's reading: the plan was a gameplay drawing, not a place, and a script fitted 374 kit meshes into rectangles by rule. Everything was deleted, and both new maps were built the other way: what the place is first, the kit's own demo map as structure, every piece looked at before use, the owner walking each version. Shortly before, he had also found he could not get past the start of that map: every dressing mesh blocked, because a collision profile set on load overrode the script's "no collision" (`0bb12e1`).
- **Misreading the owner, twice in one map.** "Do not use those assets, they are for the Valkyrie's fight" meant the lab's alien figures; the architect pulled out the whole lab kit and built another room, whose own backing box walled up its doorway (third walk). "It should work as a platform" meant "let the player get on the fallen deck tile"; the architect laid it flat on a crate, and the owner had to say "you had to leave this as it was" (sixth walk). Both are written in the blocks as the architect's mistakes; the builder now also checks walks at chest height for anything across them.
- **The robots "teleported through everything", and yesterday's smoke run had passed.** The owner: they travel 3 m in half a second towards him when he takes cover, through everything, then return to where they were, several times in two seconds. Cause: day 2's retarget had switched off root motion (it laid the characters on their backs), so each clip's travel stayed in the pelvis and the body ran ahead of its capsule and snapped back at the loop. Day 2's smoke checked that capsules moved and took still screenshots: it could not see it. The architect's first theory was wrong (one-sided collision); chasing it still found a second real fault: 1,700 trace segments on 51 kit meshes were blocked from one side only, so enemies outside a room saw and shot the player inside. Both fixed, each with a check.
- **Checks that passed broken things.** The crypt stair passed under a wall 1.3 m above the steps, and both the builder's walk check (a line at chest height) and the tour (which teleports) passed it; the owner: *"No se puede ir al sotano"* ("you can't get to the basement"). The fix brought a new check, a capsule of the player's size swept along every walk. The builder's "never blocks" list matched `SM_CastleTower_Window_A_01`..`09` by a loose name, so 207 wall pieces of the keep blocked nothing; the owner had only asked to check that no window could be passed. The tour's safety quit at 150 s silently cut off the last spots of a 154 s run.
- **Orc armor: limb pieces upside down, gauntlets on backwards.** The first fit (day 2) treated closed tubes as open plates and turned every limb piece 180 degrees. Four rounds of owner review followed ("the thumb is on the wrong side", "it is crooked", "the wrist is bent towards the little finger"). Set B's glove (a left one, a mitten nearly its own mirror image) failed three attempts, and the owner cut it: *"Sigue mal la mano, solo elimina este modelo"* ("the hand is still wrong, just delete this model").
- **The horde chief: "horrible".** Every pistol shot played a melee swing clip, and the shield hung from a guessed arm pose over his head. Owner: *"El escudo es un sinsentido, está por cualquier parte menos en su brazo"* ("the shield makes no sense, it is anywhere but on his arm"). Fixed with procedural arm IK; after the owner's next screenshot (*"aca me parece que el escudo está separado de la mano aun"*, "here the shield still looks apart from the hand") the architect measured the hand in the shield's space and found the fist 20 cm off it when lowered.
- **The pixel-art hands on the guns: five tries, then cut.** On the LAPTOP the person reviewing the AI's drawings wrote *"la mano está horrible"* ("the hand is horrible") and later *"se ve muy mal. Tienes que recrear en tu pensamiento el movimiento de una mano para agarrar una pistola"* ("it looks very bad; you have to recreate in your mind the movement of a hand gripping a pistol"). The staff hand was approved; the gun hands were not. Moved to CENTRAL, the AI drew four versions of the pistol hand, each corrected by the owner (too realistic, then held from the wrong side, then through the gun with the index off the trigger). The owner's decision: remove the hands with a gun; the 3D gun and its own animations are enough. The useful part stayed (gun pose in data, fixed weapon FOV, one bob).
- **The architect's own slips.** A PowerShell rewrite read the GDD as ANSI and broke its UTF-8 (`fb20295` repairs it). Fixed source files copied back with their old dates made the build see nothing new ("Build time: 3 s") and a test ran the old code. The architect lengthened a smoke window to 18 s without widening the arena groups' spacing, and caused nine of its own eleven errors. The castle towers were lowered 50 cm on a guess and put back when the walk check found the floor 50 cm under the walk.
- **The package is too big, and it is still open.** The kits' textures cook at 4,096 px; the zip grew from 1.2 GB to 2.6 GB during the day. Implementer: Claude Sonnet 5.5 subagents (exception, the architect's call: squad tactics need many build-and-smoke rounds and engine diagnosis, the kind of task on which Muse stalled twice on day 2). Reviews: Sol 6.1, twice.

The owner played the overnight package and wrote (12:52, Spanish, shortened): *"el tema de las IAs de los enemigos tiene demasiado trabajo que hacer [...] Unos enemigos me persiguieron por absolutamente todo el mapa [...] Ajusta la inteligencia de los enemigos para que sean inteligentes, para que se cubran [...] se dividan, busquen como rodear al jugador. Sé que ya hay técnicas para eso, en los shooters, así que por favor úsalas"* ("the enemies' AI needs a lot of work; some chased me across the whole map; make them intelligent, take cover, split up, try to surround the player; shooters already have techniques for this, please use them"). Then, in five more messages: no enemies out of nowhere, the glass wall "looks cheap", the teleporting robots described precisely, the Valkyrie with her sword, test console commands, and the horde chief (*"un orco que vas a hacer más grande [...] un escudo y una pistola especial de orco"*, "an orc you will make bigger, with a shield and a special orc pistol").

**Diagnosis first, by the architect, before any code:** the pelvis-drift table (the "teleport") and the one-sided collision grid (enemies seeing through walls), both described above.

**Built:**
- **Clips in place:** each clip's pelvis drift is measured at setup and subtracted while playing; the same measure gives each clip's true ground speed, replacing guessed values.
- **Zones, patrols, noticing:** every move goal is clamped into the encounter's zone; the first wave is in the world from the start, patrolling, guarding or dormant; the fight starts when one notices the player (sight held, gunfire heard, damage, a squadmate's alert passed along with short delays). Later waves come in at spawn points the player cannot see.
- **The squad** (pure rules, tested): roles (base of fire, two flanks, assault, anchor), a shared last known position, **attack tokens** (two or three firing at once, the rest moving), melee slots, and a position query that samples about 36 navmesh points, tests cover with traces, scores them and measures the real path for the best three.
- **The horde chief** (`AGBShieldEnemy`, scale 1.65): a shield that blocks every kind of damage in a 130-degree frontal arc while raised, a heavy pistol (one shot of 30, low rate), lowered shield only to aim; a lightning stun or a freeze knocks the shield aside.
- **The glass bulkhead**, modelled in Blender in the manner of the map's own thick windows, with eight panes that break one by one into shards (the owner: *"Quiero que cada vidrio sea independiente, y que la base del vidrio se quede"*, "each pane independent, and the frame stays"). The first pass was a wall of bright cyan clouds; it is kept in the media as the honest middle step.
- **A vault** (a third Sonnet subagent): the owner's railing could not be jumped (measured, above), so the player climbs over thin obstacles on jump or by pushing against them.
- **The Valkyrie sealed in her tube**: deaf, blind and invulnerable until the player breaks the glass. She was meant to leap out; no leap passed the landing checks in the real room, so she steps out and walks. Asked whether he wanted a real leap, the owner answered *"Así está bien"* ("it's fine as it is"; `677cf3e`).
- Console: `gb.God`, `gb.OpenValkyrie`, `gb.OpenDoors`; a test start `-AllWeapons` that the owner asked for to test the package.

**Reviews.** Sol 6.1, first pass: nine findings, none a crash, all real (the zone limited goals but not the movement; the melee permission was computed and never used; an alert from noise gave away the player's true position; the shield's check never fired a trace). Second pass on the fixes: nine more (the vault checked a tucked body but moved the full capsule, 8 cm into the ceiling; its path was not swept). All fixed, each with a check that can fail.

**A flaky check, said plainly.** Two arena checks failed one run in four because of clocks, not logic (four shooters share two tokens and 11 s was not always enough for the fourth to fire). "A check that fails one run in four is not a check": both windows were widened from measured runs.

**Final checks (17:20 to 17:50):** 65 tests, full arena smoke 0 errors (380 s), real-map smoke 0 errors (288 s), tour 85 spots, package 2,018.6 MB, packaged smokes clean.

## Design decisions
- **A place before pieces** (architect, after the owner rejected the first maps). Every space is described first (what it is, who built it, what for), the kit's own demo map is the structure, and a piece goes in only because the place asks for it. Alternative: refine the old plan's greybox; rejected with it.
- **Each map built by a script in stages** (`Build-FutureBase.py`, `Build-PastCastle.py`), never by hand: anything placed by hand is wiped by the next rebuild, and the builder carries its own checks (walks, body sweep, windows). This is also what let two chats work on one map.
- **The castle and the base are two maps joined by the seal**, not one map with two looks: the seal opens the other map after its caption, and only the progress flags cross. The smallest change that joins them.
- **Enemies: our own tactics in C++ on the runtime navmesh**, no Behavior Tree or EQS assets: the whole game stays reviewable as text, and the agents work in text. The Fab path-shaping plugin the owner offered was not installed (listed up to UE 5.8; we are on 5.9).
- **Animation without AnimBlueprint assets**: a native animation instance and proxy; procedural arms for the chief because no shield clips exist.
- **Clips from the engine's template mannequin**, then 12 clips of the Kingboars pack for patrol, crouch, sprint and the chief's one-handed pistol, imported one by one with our own script after the Fab plugin's install imported none of 1,688 files.
- **The breakable wall counts hits, not damage** (four hits, whatever each deals), so a weak weapon can open the way and a shotgun blast is one hit.
- **Pixel art must be light** (owner, GDD v0.67): the castle's pack meshes are replaced by plain-block twins rather than re-textured.
- **No hands with a gun** (owner, GDD v0.68).
- **Every first-person hand at 4 screen pixels per art pixel** (architect, before the hands were dropped): it kept the approved staff hand unscaled; scaling pixel art by 1.5 bends it.
- **Exceptions to "the implementer is Muse Spark", with the reason:** Sonnet subagents for the enemy code, tactics, vault and armor fit (many engine-specific build-and-smoke rounds; Muse stalled twice on day 2 on that kind of work; for the overnight enemies the owner allowed it); the architect directly for the breakable wall, energy gate, ladder and spell ground fix (short code tied to map work while the Editor was in use), the chief's IK (hand-written math), the seal's travel and the torch actor (about thirty and forty lines). Muse did the one task that was a clean serial batch, the gun view, with zero stalls.

## Problems and solutions
- **The Fab plugin's install of the Kingboars pack walked all 1,688 FBX files and imported none** (animation-only files without a skeleton), and closing the Editor during it crashed the Editor inside Interchange (the owner pasted the crash). Nothing was lost; the zip was taken from the plugin's temp folder and our own script imports only the 12 clips the AI uses.
