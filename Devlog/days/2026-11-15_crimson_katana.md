# 2026-11-15 - The Crimson Katana: a second sword for the future

The owner asked for a second sword so the end-game Elemental Sword is no longer the only one: a sci-fi katana from Fab, dropped by an enemy in the cyberpunk world (`FutureBase`), with the same melee logic as the existing sword, physical damage, and the weapon enchant that the guns already take. Done by a side session started by the orchestrator (Claude Code, Opus 5.5). Implementer exception: small, spread over existing files and in a hurry at the end of the jam, so the side session wrote it directly instead of briefing Muse.

## Directing: what the owner decided
- The model: the Fab listing he chose (below), shown to him in close-up renders before any in-game use (his rule). Renders: `Glyphbound/Saved/KatanaReview/` (flat colors and lit).
- Where it is found: an enemy in the future drops it, kept simple and data-only.
- How it fights: like the existing sword (`AGBSword`, the `Sword` slot), physical damage, and it can be enchanted. Then, mid-work: "the sword has to damage every enemy in front of the player, like the katanas of Cyberpunk 2077" (translated). The sword already hit every hostile inside its cone; the katana's cone was widened to 150 degrees and 3.2 m.

## How it was built (architect's decisions)
- **Data, not a new class.** `Weapons.json` gets `CrimsonKatana` (slot `Sword`, element `Physical`, damage 40 every 0.45 s, 3.2 m reach, 150-degree cone). It spawns as an `AGBSword` like the Elemental Sword.
- **Shown in 3D, like the guns, not as a pixel sprite.** The cyberpunk world's guns are 3D view models without hands (owner's rule, 2026-11-14), so the katana follows them: `Strings.ViewMesh` names its mesh and `AGBSword` builds a first-person view model from it (fixed weapon FOV, self-shadow only). Its own motion: a fast cut down to the left pivoting on the grip and a slower return (`SwingTime`). Pose numbers (`ViewScale`, `ViewOffsetX/Y/Z`, `ViewPitch/Yaw/Roll`, `ViewGripX`) are data, provisional until the owner sees it in PIE. The HUD draws no pixel blade for a sword that has a `ViewMesh`.
- **Swords take the enchant (both of them).** The enchant spell was gun-only. Now a sword in hand can cast it too (input, spellbook check, HUD hint), the hit takes the enchant's element and on-hit effect while it lasts, and the three `Enchant_*` status effects multiply `Weapon.Sword.Damage` by 1.25 as they already did for `Weapon.Gun.Damage`. Done in `.cpp` files and data only, so no Editor restart. The katana shows a light in the enchant's color on its blade; the Elemental Sword's pixel blade takes the enchant's tint.
- **Who drops it.** The Armored Orc (`Orc_Future_GunCarrier`), which already drops the first pistol. It is met in the second wave of `Future_Bay`, the transfer bay, the base's first combat hall (`Opening_Future` lists it too, but that encounter is not placed in the map; checked by reading the map's encounter ids). It now also drops the katana (chance 1). No map edit.
- **On the floor** the dropped katana shows its own model, lying flat, instead of the staff sprite other non-gun weapon pickups use.

## A mistake caught in review
The first version held the katana by the tip: the code assumed the hilt at +X, the renders showed it at -X (a yaw-90 capture camera has -X on screen-right). The session's built-in advisor (a stronger reviewer model that reads the whole session) caught it from the renders; fixed and checked with offscreen captures of the first-person pose (camera at the eye, mesh at the hand anchor, the view-model FOV), which also set the provisional pose: scale 0.08, grip 5 cm forward, 2 cm in and 8 cm up from the anchor, blade leaning 25 degrees forward. The swing could not be previewed (a human started PIE), so its amplitude is a guess.

## Where every weapon comes from in the future base (owner, later the same day)
The owner set who gives each weapon (translated): "Right where the player spawns, to his right, against the wall, where there is nothing, we put a box: find an asset already used that looks like a small box, and the player walks up and opens it like a chest. That chest gives the katana. The pistol comes from one of the first enemies in front, one of the orcs right there. The rifle from one of the enemies in the underground corridor, the robot down below. The shotgun from one of the orcs in the room before the Valkyrie, where the energy gate is." He marked the spot on a screenshot: in front of the white closed panel on the spawn room's right-hand wall.
- **Katana:** no longer an enemy drop. A chest (`AGBChest`) at the spawn, in front of the white panel (`SM_Closed_01`), holds it. It wears the bay's own cover crate (`SM_Prop_02`, High Tech Base) at 0.4 scale, about 74 cm: `AGBChest` now takes a mesh from the placed actor's Tags (`ChestMesh=<path>`, `ChestScale=<n>`), with no lid or lock and the prompt "Open crate". Placement script: `Tools/Jam/future_start_crate.py` (the map edit goes through the orchestrator, who owns the shared Editor).
- **Pistol:** the Armored Orc (`Orc_Future_GunCarrier`) moves to the first wave of `Future_Bay`, in place of the orc that stands guard, the first enemies in front of the spawn. Its old place in the second wave is a plain orc, so there is one pistol.
- **Rifle:** a new enemy id, `Robot_Future_RifleCarrier` (a copy of `Robot_Future` that drops the rifle), replaces the robot of the deep wing's first wave (`Future_DeepWing`, the underground corridor).
- **Shotgun:** the energy gate's room has no enemies placed in the saved map (`Opening_Future`, the fight meant for it, is still off), so its source waits for the owner's choice.
- **The crate is in the map.** `KatanaCrate` (an `AGBChest`, opened flag `Chest.FutureStartKatana`) stands at (-940, 1052, 0) in FutureBase, facing the room, placed with `Tools/Jam/future_start_crate.py` and saved alone (the orchestrator's go). Its mesh appears at play time (the chest builds it in BeginPlay), so the Editor shows only the actor.
- **A crash of the shared Editor, caused here.** To drop a FutureBase copy that reading it from Python had left dirty, the side session called `reload_packages` on it; the Editor stopped with "World Memory Leaks" (`EditorServer.cpp:1950`), because those Python reads still held references to the map's actors. Nothing was saved by that step; an unrelated unsaved material (`MI_R6`) was lost. The orchestrator restarted the Editor. Lesson: read a map that is not open only inside a function (no lingering references), and never reload a world package from Python.
- **Shotgun (owner's choice: "the fight in the room before the held Valkyrie, where the gate to unlock is").** A new id, `Orc_Future_ShotgunCarrier` (the Armored Orc with the shotgun), stands guard in `Opening_Future`, which is now prespawned (him and two orcs, then two drones and two orcs; 1 skill point, so 44 are earnable). `Tools/Jam/future_gate_fight.py` measured the lab room with traces (about 21 x 21 m, a dais at the gate 0.5 m up) and placed the encounter `GateRoomFight` at its centre: six spawn points, all checked onto open floor, a trigger over the whole room, era presence copied from `Future_Bay`. FutureBase was saved alone, with a dated backup taken first, while the orchestrator held the final-boss agent and the owner's PIE out of the shared Editor; all Python ran inside functions after the earlier crash.

## The katana in the owner's hands: position and a three-cut combo
After his first PIE with it, the owner drew where the katana should sit (lower and further right) and asked for weight in the swing (translated): "a beginning with weight, the slash super fast, and the speed letting itself go at the end", "a combo of three: a diagonal slash from right to left, the second, right after, from left to right, and a third from right to left but horizontal, the katana a little raised, like in Cyberpunk 2077", and "the sword must not stay fixed while it cuts, it needs some movement".
- **Position, solved, not guessed.** A small projection of the katana's guard and tip (the game's view-model FOV, 58.72 degrees, and the hand anchor) matched his screenshot within a few pixels, taking the crosshair as the centre. A search over offsets and lean then fitted his yellow band: grip 15 cm forward, 6 cm right and 5 cm down from the anchor, blade leaning 35 degrees forward and 15 degrees towards the centre (data in `Weapons.json`).
- **The combo** (`AGBSword`, `.cpp` only, so no restart): six keyframes (wind-up and end of each cut) solved the same way against screen targets for the tip and guard. Each cut is a slow eased wind-up (0.10 s), a very fast strike (0.05 s) during which the blade also swings out 7 cm along its arc, a follow-through that carries 12% past the end and dies out (0.11 s), and a return (0.24 s). Quaternion slerp keeps each cut on its short arc. A press within the fire interval plus 0.35 s goes on to the next cut, otherwise the combo starts again. At rest the blade sways slowly.
- **Third cut, level (owner):** "in the third hit lay the katana down horizontally and make the cut from right to left horizontally too" (translated). The wind-up was solved so the blade lies horizontal on the right, a little above the screen centre; the end is that same pose turned 80 degrees only about the vertical axis, so the slerp (and the follow-through) keeps the blade level across the whole screen. Checked with the projection: the tip stays at the same height from right to left.
- **"Nothing changed" (owner), and why.** The level third cut compiled fine but did not show. Live Coding patches code but keeps the storage of global variables that already exist in the running Editor, so the combo's keyframe table (a dynamically initialised global) kept the values of the first patch. The keys are now built inside a function on each call, so a Live Coding compile applies them. Lesson for tuning through Live Coding: values that must change live go in function bodies or data files, not in namespace-scope tables.
