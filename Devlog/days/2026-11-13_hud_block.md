## The owner's request
The owner's prompt (Spanish): *"Vamos a hacer un rework completo de la interfaz y el hud. Funciona bien la actual, pero es basica y fea. Verás que son 2 estilos. Ambos tienen que captar la escencia de su respectivo estilo. Para lo cyberpunk, ten de referencia a cyberpunk 2077. Cuando me vayas a entregar resultados, toma captura de todo lo hecho, míralo tú mismo, juzga tú mismo, corrige lo que consideres de corregir, y así sucesivamente hasta que de verdad estés muy contento con el resultado. Quiero que se vea muy bien, exigente."*

Translation: "Let's do a complete rework of the interface and the HUD. The current one works, but it is basic and ugly. You will see there are 2 styles. Both have to capture the essence of their style. For the cyberpunk one, use Cyberpunk 2077 as the reference. When you deliver, take screenshots of everything, look at them yourself, judge them yourself, fix what you think needs fixing, and so on until you are really happy with the result. I want it to look very good; be demanding."

GDD §13 already had the rule: the HUD follows the held item (staff and sword = 1990s first-person RPG, gun = modern cyberpunk), the spell tree is always RPG and the weapon tree always cyberpunk. What existed was one set of flat rectangles and the engine's default font with two color sets.

## Decisions (architect) and why
| Decision | Why |
|---|---|
| Implemented by the architect chat itself, not by Muse Spark (an exception to the implementer rule) | The work is visual design judged screenshot by screenshot: every round was "look, decide, change twenty small numbers, look again". Writing a brief for each of those rounds would have cost more than doing them, and the owner asked the architect to judge the result. |
| Still drawn on the HUD canvas, no UMG | All the UI already lived in `AGBHUD` with mouse, keyboard and gamepad handling; a canvas keeps everything in C++ and text in git, with no widget assets to import or cook. |
| The RPG skin is laid out on a grid of whole art pixels (`ArtPixel()`: 4 screen pixels at 1080p, 3 at 720p) | Pixel art only reads as pixel art if every edge is a whole sprite pixel. Frames, bevels, rivets, gems, tubes and the cursor are drawn as blocks of that size, not as textures that get filtered. |
| A pixel font of our own (`UI/GBPixelFont.*`), 5 x 7 capitals with lowercase and descenders, drawn as text rows in code | No font asset to license or import, and a 16-bit look no engine font has. It is baked at runtime into atlases with a vertical color ramp per "ink" (gold, bone, blood, mana, ice, ember) plus an outline atlas. |
| The cyberpunk type is the engine's own Roboto (Bold Condensed, Regular, Medium) and Droid Sans Mono, loaded from `Engine/Content/Slate/Fonts` | Licensed with the engine (Apache 2.0) and always staged in a packaged build (checked in the build manifest). Condensed capitals with letter spacing carry most of the Cyberpunk 2077 feel. Nothing was downloaded. |
| Menus follow the held item's skin (GDD §13 had the inventory's look as TBD) | The style switch is the game's signature (GDD §14); a menu in the other style than the HUD under it would break it. Provisional, in the GDD. |
| One menu layout for both skins (list left, detail right, key hints in the footer), with skin-neutral widgets (`MenuRow`, `MenuDetailPanel`, `MenuHints`, `UiText` roles) | One set of menu logic to keep correct (the mouse hit areas of every row, tab and button are unchanged), two looks. |
| Ten 16 x 16 spell icons drawn by the architect in `Sprites.json` | The sockets were text ("L.Bolt"); a 90s RPG reads by icons. Drawn in the master palette and previewed at 8x before going in (`media/2026-11-13/hud_spell_icons.png`). |
| A smoke block `gb.Smoke ui` that walks through every screen in both skins and takes 22 screenshots; `Smoke-Jam.ps1` got `-ResX` / `-ResY` | The owner asked for screenshots of everything. The block runs offscreen in 45 s, so each round of changes could be judged at 1920 x 1080 and at 1280 x 720. |

## The rounds of self-review (what was wrong each time, and the fix)
1. **First run:** the RPG skin looked right at once; the cyberpunk skin drew boxes but **no text at all**. Cause: the engine's canvas only draws text with a Slate font when the text item also carries a UFont of the runtime font cache, and drops it silently otherwise. Fix: a hard reference to `/Engine/EngineFonts/Roboto` (also so it is always cooked) set on every item.
2. **Second run:** RPG: the "DASH" tube fell off the bottom of the screen; the toasts covered the inventory tabs; two-line skill names ran into the rank gems; the hands hid behind the new bar (now they stand on it). Cyberpunk: hard-edged dark rectangles behind the corners (replaced by soft shadows); the weapon block was cramped and a rule ran through "ENCHANTED"; the QTE labels were unreadable over the bright future level; small text in a monospace font looked like debug output (now Roboto Medium; mono only for technical labels); the detail hints read "Enter or click: use" (now a key box and an action, like the footer).
3. **Third run:** the cyberpunk enchant module sat on top of the pistol model, where nothing reads; the whole weapon block became a narrow column at the right edge.
4. **720p run:** the "DASH" label was cut by the screen edge (moved to the end of its tube) and the RPG skill names did not fit two lines (tighter node frame, smaller gaps; one abbreviated line only if two still do not fit; the cost became a small badge on the node's corner).

## Review
Sol 6.1 (Codex CLI, medium, read-only) reviewed the change: prompt `Prompts/hud_rework_review_sol.md`, run `Devlog/raw/CENTRAL/2026-11-13/agents/123236-codex-3c53f0/`.
- No findings on crashes, garbage collection, packaging (it confirmed that UE 5.9 stages the Slate TTF fonts whenever a target uses Slate, and that the Roboto hard reference covers the runtime font) or per-frame cost. Mouse hit areas, keyboard confirm and the death-screen respawn: unchanged.
- One finding, accepted and fixed: in menus the toasts were anchored at 80 % of the screen height and grew downward, so at 1280 x 720 the fourth and fifth queued messages fell below the screen. The stack now moves up as it grows.

A new test, `Glyphbound.UI.PixelFont`: every character the UI uses has a glyph, every glyph is rectangular and at most 5 x 9, capitals and digits are 7 rows tall, and text width is the glyph widths plus one spacing column (a mistyped glyph row would shift the whole atlas and nobody would notice while playing).
